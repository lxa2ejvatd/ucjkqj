What Is GLP-1?

Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) is a hormone released by intestinal cells in response to eating. Key points:

Origin: Produced by L-cells in the small intestine.

Stimulates insulin secretion when blood glucose is high.

Slows gastric emptying (food moves more slowly from stomach to intestine).

Signals satiety (fullness) to the brain.

GLP-1 receptors (GLP-1R) are found in pancreatic cells, the gastrointestinal tract, and crucially, in areas of the brain that regulate appetite and reward.

How GLP-1 Regulates Appetite

Peripheral Actions

Delayed gastric emptying• Slower nutrient delivery to the small intestine reduces post-meal blood sugar spikes and prolongs feelings of fullness.

Insulin release• Enhanced insulin response helps control blood sugar, reducing hunger triggered by rapid glucose swings.

Central Nervous System Actions

Hypothalamus activation• GLP-1R in the arcuate nucleus promote anorexigenic (appetite-suppressing) signals.

Brainstem involvement• GLP-1 influences the nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS), integrating gut-brain satiety signals via the vagus nerve.

Reward centers• GLP-1 receptors in the ventral tegmental area (VTA) and nucleus accumbens modulate dopamine release, dampening the pleasure response to high-calorie foods.

GLP-1 Impact on Cravings

Biochemical Pathways

Receptor bindingGLP-1 binds to GLP-1R, a G-protein coupled receptor, activating adenylate cyclase.

cAMP cascadeIncreased cyclic AMP (cAMP) and protein kinase A (PKA) activity alter neuronal firing rates.

Neurotransmitter modulationReduced release of orexigenic (hunger-promoting) neuropeptides like NPY/AgRP; increased anorexigenic peptides such as POMC/CART.

Neural Circuit Effects

Reduced reward-driven eatingBy attenuating dopamine surges in response to palatable foods, GLP-1 signaling lowers the motivational drive to seek out sugary or fatty items.

Enhanced satiety signalsStrengthening of vagal afferent feedback to the brainstem reduces the perceived need to eat, even in the presence of food cues.

Clinical Evidence

Studies in Nature Medicine and The Lancet demonstrate that GLP-1 analogs (see next section) reduce caloric intake by up to 15–20% compared to placebo.

Functional MRI trials show decreased activation in reward-related brain regions when subjects on GLP-1 therapy view images of high-calorie foods.

Real-world data confirm sustained weight loss over 1–2 years, largely driven by reduced binge episodes and snacking frequency.
