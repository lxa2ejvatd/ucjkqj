Internal Medicine

Narcolepsy is a chronic neurological sleep disorder that occurs when the immune system mistakenly attacks brain cells responsible for regulating sleep-wake cycles. This autoimmune response is often triggered by a combination of genetic predisposition and environmental factors, such as viral or bacterial infections. The result is a deficiency in hypocretin, a brain chemical that helps keep you awake.

Because narcolepsy symptoms—like excessive daytime sleepiness, sudden muscle weakness, and sleep paralysis—can overlap with many other conditions, identifying the cause of your symptoms early is critical. Explanation

Narcolepsy is a condition that makes people very sleepy during the day. It happens when the brain doesn't make enough of a chemical called hypocretin, which helps control when we sleep and stay awake. Scientists believe that narcolepsy can be caused by the body's immune system attacking the cells that produce hypocretin.
