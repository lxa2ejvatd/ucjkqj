Explanation

Cloves are more than a fragrant kitchen spice. These dried flower buds from the Syzygium aromaticum tree have been used for centuries in traditional medicine to ease pain, reduce inflammation, and support overall wellness. Today, modern research is beginning to confirm what many cultures have long believed: cloves contain powerful bioactive compounds that may help with certain types of pain and inflammatory conditions.

Below is a science-based, practical guide to how cloves work, what the research shows, and how to use them safely.

What Makes Cloves Medicinal?

The main active compound in cloves is eugenol, which makes up 70–90% of clove essential oil. Eugenol is responsible for many of the spice's therapeutic effects.

Cloves also contain:

Flavonoids – plant compounds with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects

Tannins – natural compounds that may reduce inflammation

Beta-caryophyllene – a compound with potential pain-modulating properties

Vitamins and minerals – including manganese and vitamin K

These compounds work together to produce antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and mild anesthetic effects.

Cloves and Pain Relief: What the Science Says

1. Dental Pain (Most Supported Use)

One of the best-supported uses of cloves is for toothache and gum pain.

Eugenol acts as:

A natural anesthetic, numbing nerve endings

An anti-inflammatory agent, reducing swelling

An antimicrobial, helping control oral bacteria

Dentists have long used eugenol in temporary fillings and dental cements. Studies show it can reduce dental pain when applied topically.

How it's used:

A small amount of diluted clove oil applied to the affected area

Whole cloves gently pressed near the painful tooth

Important: Clove oil is strong and can irritate gums if used undiluted or in large amounts.

2. Muscle Pain and Myalgia

Cloves may help with muscle soreness and myalgia (muscle pain) because of their anti-inflammatory and circulation-supporting properties.

Research suggests eugenol may:

Inhibit inflammatory pathways (such as COX enzymes)

Reduce oxidative stress in tissues

Support blood flow when applied topically

Some topical balms include clove oil for muscle discomfort. However, evidence in humans is still limited compared to dental uses.

If you're experiencing ongoing or unexplained muscle discomfort and want to better understand what might be causing it, you can take a quick symptom assessment to explore potential causes and receive personalized guidance on your next steps. Persistent or severe muscle pain should always be evaluated by a medical professional.

3. Joint Pain and Inflammation

Chronic inflammation plays a central role in joint pain, including osteoarthritis.

Laboratory and animal studies show that compounds in cloves may:

Reduce inflammatory markers

Decrease nitric oxide production (a contributor to inflammation)

Lower oxidative stress in joint tissues

While early findings are promising, large-scale human clinical trials are still limited. Cloves may be a supportive addition to an overall anti-inflammatory lifestyle, but they are not a substitute for prescribed arthritis treatments.

4. Headaches

Clove oil has traditionally been used for tension-type headaches.

Possible mechanisms include:

Mild numbing effect

Muscle relaxation

Anti-inflammatory activity

Some people apply diluted clove oil to the temples. However, strong oils can irritate the skin and eyes, so dilution with a carrier oil (like coconut oil) is essential.

If headaches are severe, sudden, or accompanied by symptoms such as vision changes, weakness, confusion, or fever, seek immediate medical care.

Cloves as an Anti-Inflammatory Agent

Inflammation is a normal immune response. But chronic inflammation can contribute to:

Arthritis

Heart disease

Diabetes

Autoimmune disorders

Cloves are rich in antioxidants, which help neutralize free radicals that drive inflammation.

Key Anti-Inflammatory Mechanisms:

Suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokines

Reduction in oxidative stress

Inhibition of certain inflammatory enzymes

While cloves show measurable anti-inflammatory effects in laboratory settings, they should be viewed as a supportive wellness tool, not a cure.

Additional Health Benefits of Cloves

Beyond pain and inflammation, cloves may offer other potential benefits.

1. Antimicrobial Properties

Cloves have been shown to inhibit growth of certain bacteria and fungi. This supports their traditional use in oral care and food preservation.

2. Digestive Support

In traditional medicine, cloves are used to:

Reduce bloating

Support digestion

Ease nausea

Some evidence suggests cloves may increase digestive enzyme secretion.

3. Blood Sugar Regulation

Preliminary studies suggest clove extracts may improve insulin function and lower blood sugar levels. However, more human research is needed.

If you have diabetes, do not replace prescribed medications with cloves. Always consult your doctor before making changes.

Cloves are generally safe when used in food. Concentrated forms require more caution.

1. Whole or Ground Cloves (Culinary Use)

Added to teas

Used in cooking

Sprinkled into warm beverages

Risks and Precautions

Cloves are natural, but "natural" does not mean risk-free.

Potential Risks:

Skin irritation or burns (especially undiluted oil)

Allergic reactions

Increased bleeding risk (eugenol may slow blood clotting)

Liver toxicity at very high doses

People who should speak with a doctor before using concentrated clove products:

Those on blood thinners

People with bleeding disorders

Individuals with liver disease

Pregnant or breastfeeding women

Children (clove oil can be dangerous if swallowed)

If you experience symptoms such as severe abdominal pain, yellowing of the skin, unusual bruising, or allergic reactions, seek medical care promptly.

Realistic Expectations

Cloves can support wellness, but they are not a miracle cure.

What they can do:

Provide mild topical pain relief

Offer antioxidant support

Contribute to an anti-inflammatory diet

What they cannot do:

Replace medical treatment for serious infections

Cure arthritis

Eliminate chronic disease

Substitute for professional care in severe pain

If pain is persistent, worsening, or interfering with daily life, it's important to speak to a doctor. Some causes of pain and inflammation can be serious or even life-threatening if left untreated.

The Bottom Line

Cloves are a powerful medicinal spice backed by both tradition and emerging science. Their active compound, eugenol, gives them measurable pain-relieving and anti-inflammatory effects—especially for dental discomfort and possibly for muscle and joint pain.

Used properly, cloves can be:

A helpful addition to a balanced wellness plan

A natural option for mild, temporary pain relief

A flavorful way to boost antioxidant intake

However, they should be used thoughtfully and safely. Chronic pain, severe inflammation, or unexplained symptoms deserve medical evaluation.
