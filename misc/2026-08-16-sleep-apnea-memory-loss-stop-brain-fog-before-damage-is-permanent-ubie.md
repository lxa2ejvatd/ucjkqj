Can sleep apnea cause memory loss or brain fog?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleep apnea can cause memory loss, brain fog, and difficulty concentrating. Repeated breathing interruptions during sleep reduce oxygen flow to the brain and fragment restorative sleep, impairing memory consolidation, focus, and cognitive performance over time. Untreated sleep apnea is also linked to a higher long-term risk of dementia.

Explanation

Sleep apnea is a condition where breathing stops and starts during sleep, leading to poor sleep quality. This can affect how well the brain works, causing problems like memory loss and brain fog.

How Sleep Apnea Affects the Brain

[Brain Fog](https://lumina-dermis.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/7113851468.html)

Memory Problems: People with sleep apnea might have trouble remembering things. This happens because the brain doesn't get enough rest during sleep, making it hard to store and recall information.

[Doctor's Note](https://peplawllc.com/blog/1337773415.html)

Brain Fog: Sleep apnea can make you feel confused or unable to think clearly. This is often called 'brain fog'. It's like when you feel groggy and can't focus after not getting enough sleep.

Why This Happens

Interrupted Sleep: Sleep apnea causes you to wake up many times during the night, even if you don't remember it. This prevents deep, restful sleep.
