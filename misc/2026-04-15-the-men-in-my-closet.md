The oldest totem in my closet is a Stone Harbor Beach Patrol T-shirt, circa 1979, stolen from a Volkswagen Bug that belonged to a lifeguard named David, who I was hoping would ask me to the End of Summer Ball. And there is a blue-and-yellow Hawaiian shirt that belonged to a man named Shelton, whom I secretly/not-so-secretly loved for a decade. I also have Kevin's black-and-white rag-wool sweater, though I never had Kevin the way I wanted him. Not even close.

If I'm honest about it, a good 25 percent of my closet is taken up by clothing that used to belong to the men I loved, or the men I have not quite been allowed to. Wearing their shirts, their socks, their button-fly Levi's, is a way to keep these men close to my heart, when life, or sometimes even death, has taken them elsewhere. Slipping into each piece of clothing brings back not only the man from my past, but also who I was and the places we went when I was with him.

And when love is brand new--that terrible, glorious state--isn't there part of every woman (the impractical, apolitical part) that wants nothing more than to be wrapped in the arms of her beloved? And if it is really love, won't that time of wanting almost certainly coincide with the time the man is completely freaking out, wanting only to be alone? Doesn't it make sense, then, for a woman to wrap herself in his smell and his laundry soap, in his silks, cottons, and denims, and wait for the day he is ready to calm down and come around?
