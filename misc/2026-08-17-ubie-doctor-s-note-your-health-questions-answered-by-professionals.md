What criteria are used to diagnose narcolepsy?

Narcolepsy diagnosis typically involves three key steps: 1. **Medical history review** — Your healthcare provider will ask detailed questions about your sleep patterns, daytime sleepiness, and overall health. 2. **Physical examination** — A full physical exam helps rule out other conditions that may cause similar symptoms. 3. **Sleep studies** — If narcolepsy is suspected, two specialized tests are commonly ordered together: - **Polysomnography (PSG):** An overnight sleep study conducted in a specialized facility that monitors brain activity, breathing, and movement. - **Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT):** Performed the following morning, this daytime test measures how quickly you fall asleep in a quiet setting. Together, these evaluations help confirm narcolepsy and rule out other sleep disorders. Because excessive daytime sleepiness can stem from many different conditions—not just narcolepsy—identifying the right cause early is critical to getting effective treatment. What diseases are commonly associated with drooping eyelids?

Drooping eyelids, medically known as ptosis, occur when the upper eyelid falls lower than normal over the eye. Ptosis can affect one or both eyes and may develop suddenly or gradually over time.
