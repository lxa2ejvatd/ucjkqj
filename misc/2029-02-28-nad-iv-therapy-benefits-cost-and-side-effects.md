What is NAD+?

NAD+, short for nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, is a coenzyme found in every cell in the body and plays a crucial role in cellular metabolism and energy production. It is a molecule composed of two nucleotides, nicotinamide and adenosine. Because it has a double-stranded structure, it is called a dinucleotide.

NAD+ is naturally occurring in the body and is formed from the essential nutrient niacin, also known as vitamin B3, through a process known as a nicotinamide salvage pathway. Cellular respiration acts as a “carrier” for electrons, allowing energy-rich molecules, like glucose, to send their electrons to the energy-making mitochondria. This transfer is essential for producing ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the body’s primary energy source.

As we age, the concentration of NAD+ levels in the body decreases, leading to a decline in energy production and cellular function.

Various factors, including oxidative stress, inflammation, poor diet, and lifestyle choices like excessive alcohol consumption, smoking, and a lack of physical activity, can cause this decrease in NAD+ levels.

How does NAD+ work?

NAD+ is found in all living cells and plays a crucial role in many biological processes in the body. It is involved in energy metabolism, DNA repair, and cellular communication.

The body uses NAD+ to maneuver electrons and energy along different metabolic pathways. It takes electrons from metabolic intermediates and gives them to the electron transport chain in the mitochondria, a process called oxidative metabolism. This process makes ATP, a form of energy cells that accomplish many different bodily functions.

NAD+ also plays a role in maintaining the integrity of DNA, as it is involved in DNA repair pathways, helping prevent genetic mutations and protecting against cell damage and deterioration. NAD+ also helps control circadian rhythms and keep sirtuins working. Sirtuins are a group of proteins that play a role in aging and living longer.

Supplementing NAD+ can positively affect health and wellness, including better energy production, communication between cells, and DNA repair. NAD+ IV infusions are more bioavailable than other forms of NAD, and they can effectively raise the amount of NAD+ in the body.
