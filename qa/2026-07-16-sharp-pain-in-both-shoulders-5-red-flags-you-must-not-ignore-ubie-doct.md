Sharp pain in both shoulders may be a sign of neuralgic amyotrophy, a nerve disorder that causes sudden, severe shoulder pain followed by muscle weakness. This condition affects the brachial plexus—a network of nerves controlling the shoulders and arms—and often begins abruptly, sometimes after an illness, injury, or vaccination. While neuralgic amyotrophy is one possible cause, sharp bilateral shoulder pain can also stem from rotator cuff issues, arthritis, pinched nerves, or referred pain from other organs.

Explanation

Sharp pain in both shoulders may be due to a rare condition called neuralgic amyotrophy, which stems from nerve issues. This condition can cause severe pain and muscle weakness in the shoulders.

Key Points:

Neuralgic Amyotrophy: This is a nerve disorder that can cause sudden and sharp pain in the shoulders. It may lead to muscle weakness and can affect both shoulders.

Symptoms: People with neuralgic amyotrophy often experience intense pain, followed by weakness in the shoulder muscles. This can make it hard to lift the arms or carry things.

Cause: The exact cause of neuralgic amyotrophy is not well understood, but it may be linked to infections or immune system problems.
