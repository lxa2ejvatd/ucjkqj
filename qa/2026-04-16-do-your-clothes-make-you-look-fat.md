# Do Your Clothes Make You Look Fat?

The defensive linemen in the NFL are pissed off—and rightfully so: their new uniforms make them look fat.

The big guys you watch on Sundays voiced their concerns about their Nike uniforms—new to the league this year after switching from a Reebok wardrobe—in a recent Wall Street Journal article. The gist: A “body-contoured fit” isn’t exactly the most flattering option for 300-pound linemen.

Your Crime: Dorky Jeans Maybe you don’t even realize it, but if your jeans are baggy, stiff, and they hit you above the waist, you’re guilty. Another mistake: “Denim on denim on denim,” says style expert Tom Julian, author of the Nordstrom Guide to Men's Style. “It’s not designed to be a uniform or suit.”

Your Crime: An Ill-Fitting Suit Jacket sleeves too long? Pant hems dragging below the top of the back of your heel? Your suit doesn’t fit—let alone flatter your silhouette, says Julian. And one thing that all successful men know is that even if you're not sure, you might as well look like you are.

Your fix: The sleeves of your sports coat should fall around your wrists, allowing ¼ inch of the shirt cuff to show. Also, choose slim fit shirts to flatter your frame—even if you don’t have a six-pack—instead of adding unneeded bulk.

Your crime: A Chunky, Multi-Colored Sweater OK, so Bill Cosby (somehow) pulled off the multi-colored, multi-textured, oversized sweater in the '80s. But that doesn’t mean you should try to do the same, Julian says.

Your fix: Don't mix crazy texture with crazy patterns—choose one or the other. “Merino wool sweaters are great investments,” says Julian. And if you’re an active guy, the placket style works well since it functions as a pullover, too. (Try this one from Banana Republic.) Cable knits and textured rib knits are also great—just stay to one color or tone, he adds.
