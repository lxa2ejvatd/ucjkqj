# How to Stop an Opioid Addiction Before It Starts

[Stop an Opioid](https://healingkinetics.bbsxyxy.com/blog/2776357917.html)

Opioid abuse was declared a public-health emergency in 2017, and the number of opioid-related deaths has spiked since the pandemic. Prescription pain-killers are only part of the issue; people seeking the same relief regularly turn to other (often cheaper and more available) options, including heroin and fake prescription pills. These are frequently laced with the far more potent drug fentanyl; last year, the DEA seized enough to give every American a lethal dose. Use these steps to avoid or escape addiction.

Ask for an alternative prescription

Four out of five new heroin users start out by misusing prescription pain-killers. Addiction can happen easily. One recent study found that teens and young adults who received opioid prescriptions after a dental procedure were at risk of developing an addiction to them the following year. If you are prescribed an opioid painkiller, ask if there is an alternative. If not, see if you can use it for three or fewer days, because that reduces your risk of addiction.

Reverse an overdose

If you are using drugs like prescription painkillers, heroin, codeine, and fentanyl, or you know someone who is, goto the pharmacy and get Narcan nasal spray. It contains naloxone, a drug that can reverse an opioid overdose. Also be aware that there are treatments available to help you quit, says Edwin Salsitz, M.D., an addiction specialist at Mount Sinai Hospital. Medications like methadone and buprenorphine decrease withdrawal symptoms and cravings. Naltrexone takes away the satisfaction of using and helps prevent relapse after you’ve quit.

Get anonymous help

Behavioral therapy can be effective intreating addiction disorders; however, finding that type of support can be daunting. If you’re not ready to bring it up to a doctor, you have other options. You can use an online support group on Reddit or the research-based group SMART Recovery as a more anonymous means of getting help. “A lot of clergy know a lot about this aswell,” says Dr. Salsitz.
