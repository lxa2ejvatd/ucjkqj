The Difference Between Self-Esteem and Body Image

Body image is how you think and feel about your body. Another way to describe it is the evaluation of your physical appearance, especially regarding how you think others may perceive you.

Conversely, self-esteem is the way you feel about yourself as a person. It's how you see yourself and how much value you place in your existence. Self-esteem can take into account many different factors related to life in general.

The primary differences between self-esteem and body image are that body image refers only to how you see yourself, while self-esteem can be a more complex concept that considers your thoughts and feelings about the world around you.

The similarities between self-esteem and body image are that they both focus on how you feel about yourself. As a result, they both can affect how you behave and interact with others. Moreover, they are both natural human considerations.

Negative Body Image and Self-Esteem

A negative body image can stem from a variety of issues ranging from unrealistic expectations to body dysmorphia. Overall, having a negative body image can damage your self-esteem and can lead to depression or other mental health conditions.

Notably, a positive attitude is key. Negative thinking can negatively impact both physical and mental health. Anything from family remarks to unrealistic body types on social media may influence people’s body image.

Signs of a Negative Body Image

Though the signs of a negative body image are not always easy to identify, there are some ways you can tell if someone is struggling. If you notice any of these signs in yourself or a loved one, it might be time to take action:

Negative thoughts that focus on body weight and appearance as the reason for failure.

Inability to feel comfortable no matter what they wear or how they look.

Refusal to go out in public if their body looks a certain way.

A lack of interest or ability to be intimate because they are concerned about others seeing their bodies.

Negative thoughts that lead to negative behaviors, such as avoiding social situations where weight is at the forefront (for example, going out with friends)

If you notice any of these, it's time to work on the root of the issue. Negative thoughts and actions can have a detrimental impact on your mental and physical health and, consequently, on your relationships. This might result in intimacy issues that require medication to help you regain comfort and confidence in being intimate with your partner.

For some, it might be challenging to talk about these problems on your own. However, with the help of a good counselor, you can overcome negative thoughts and behaviors.

Making an Escape From Negative Body Image and Low Self-Esteem

There are always methods to elevate oneself — consider the following for ideas:

Be around people who support you, as opposed to tearing you down.

Work on loving yourself no matter what other people may say or think about your body.

Try to engage in a hobby that makes you feel great rather than focusing solely on weight-loss goals (for example, going for a run after work instead of drinking with friends).

Create goals that have nothing to do with remedying the physical attribute that bothers you.

Incorporate simple practices into your daily routine, such as getting enough sleep and being kind to yourself in the process.

Negative thoughts and behaviors can negatively impact your health in both the short and long term. If you want to live a happy life free of anxiety, depression, or intimacy issues, then seek the help you deserve sooner than later. Everyone deserves to live a happy life.
